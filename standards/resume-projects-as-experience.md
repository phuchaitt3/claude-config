# Resume: presenting projects as experience

**Question:** With no formal work history, should projects be formatted like jobs, and should they carry job titles such as "Machine Learning Engineer"?

**Finding:**

- **Format: yes.** Career guidance agrees that projects can stand in for work history, written like job entries: name, context, dates, accomplishment bullets. Those entries normally sit under a "Projects" heading, or a heading renamed to match the work.
- **Projects are valued, and so are internships.** In CodePath's survey of 222 engineering leaders (Aug-Sep 2025), the top readiness signals outside the interview were side projects or portfolios (38%), internships (35%) and public code such as GitHub (34%). Respondents could pick several, so 38% against 35% is close to a tie, and it is not a ranking of hiring criteria.
- **Skills-based hiring is real, but says nothing about labels.** 70% of employers in NACE's Job Outlook 2026 survey use it for entry-level hires, up from 65%. The same release repeats the value of internships or work during college.
- **A bare "Experience" heading for non-employment: one guide advises against it.** Carnegie Mellon's Washington Semester guide says the standalone title "implies employment" and suggests renaming the section to fit the entries (see `thesis-as-research-experience`). This is the only source found on the point. The ATS sources treat "Experience" as safe for parsing, which is a separate question.
- **Job titles on projects: no direct evidence either way.** No source found addresses this exact case, though Binghamton accepts "undergraduate or graduate researcher" for class projects. Advice on titles for paid jobs is the nearest: hiring managers may verify titles, recruiters say inflated ones make a story "less credible", and the suggested fix is to add context ("CTO at a six-person startup") rather than drop the title.
- **A lead title on a class project: normal, with the team size beside it** (added 2026-11-09). University career centres show it in their own samples: RPI's resume guide lists a course project with the role "Project Team Leader" and the bullet "Led and collaborated with a team of 6 classmates", and tells students to give "the number of classmates on your team, if you took a lead role". Bowie State's project template also asks for the number of team members. Commercial guides add: name the course, give the team size, and be ready to say what you did as lead. No source calls a lead title on a class project inflated. The inflation warnings above are about job-sounding titles, which a role like "Team Lead" next to "Course Project" avoids.
- **Background checks:** evidence that a title mismatch costs an offer is anecdotal. Fisher Phillips, a law firm, lists a mismatch between resume and verification among reasons an employer might pull an offer.
- Checker's inference: a role-style title with the context stated next to it ("team project") fits both the format advice and the title advice.

**Limits:** Most sources are US-based; none covers Vietnam or how large employers there treat this. The title advice concerns paid jobs and is applied to projects by analogy. No study measures how recruiters respond to job titles on projects. Claims seen but not supported by any source found: that recruiters do not care whether work was paid, that "academic project" labels get discounted, that an ATS skips projects not formatted as jobs, and that project work earns the same respect as an internship.
