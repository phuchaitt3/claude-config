# ATS and resume section headings

**Question:** Does an applicant tracking system (ATS) look for the exact heading "Work Experience", and does a resume without it get rejected?

**Finding:**

- No source shows rejection for a missing or differently worded heading. Even a resume-tool vendor says many ATS do not reject on headings alone; the risk it names is bad parsing, which hurts searchability.
- "Experience" is listed as an accepted variant of "Work Experience", alongside "Professional Experience" and "Employment History". Custom headings ("My Journey") are the ones warned against.
- "Projects" is a recognised heading, and the usual substitute when a candidate has no work history. Entries are formatted like job entries.
- Automatic rejection in general is rare: in a 25-recruiter study, 92% said their systems do not auto-reject on formatting, design, missing keywords or low match scores. The filters that do exist are hard requirements such as work authorisation, licences, minimum qualifications and location.
- Checker's inference: a heading containing "Experience", such as "Research Experience", should map like "Experience". No source tests this.

**Sources:**

- [IT Brief on the Enhancv study](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/study-reveals-ats-rarely-auto-rejects-cvs-debunks-75-myth) (trade press reporting a vendor study, 2025-11-19).
- [JobShinobi, section headings](https://jobshinobi.com/blog/ats-optimized-resume-section-headings-that-parse) (resume-tool vendor blog; paraphrases Workday, Workable and SmartRecruiters material without linking it).
- [Rezi, parse failures](https://www.rezi.ai/posts/why-your-ats-parse-failed) (resume-tool vendor blog; read through a search summary only).
- [Teal, Workday resumes](https://www.tealhq.com/post/workday-resume) (resume-tool vendor blog; found in search, not read in full).

**Limits:** No primary vendor documentation or independent test of parser behaviour was found, and none specific to Workday. Almost every source sells resume help, so the sources have a commercial interest; some blogs claim the opposite with no evidence. The study has 25 respondents, chosen by a method the coverage does not describe, so its percentages are indicative only. A candidate can see what a given system parsed by checking its autofill.
