# Must a one-page resume fill the page?

- **Question:** When a tailored one-page resume has free space after the irrelevant bullets are cut, should the space be filled (with bullets cut earlier) or left empty?
- **Finding:**
  - **Not required to fill it.** Sources agree that some white space is good for skimming; the problem is a *large* empty area (about a third of the page or more) that suggests thin experience, not a small gap at the bottom.
  - **Fill with relevant substance only.** For entry-level candidates, the advice is to add real, relevant material (projects, coursework, tools) when there is a big gap. Where nothing relevant is left, a shorter, cleanly spaced page beats padding.
  - **Do not stretch the layout.** Wide margins, oversized headings or fonts under 10 pt to adjust length are named as signs of a stretched page; treat page fit as a content decision, not a spacing one.
  - **Cut for relevance, not for length.** Recruiter-backed guides say to remove achievements that do not support the target role even if impressive elsewhere, and to write for a 10-second scan. None recommends adding off-topic bullets to reach the bottom margin.
  - **Page limit:** one page is the common norm for 0-5 years of experience; a minority of sources say two pages are accepted. Evidence is thin.
  - *Checker's inference:* a page that is about 85-95% full is normal and needs no action. Restore a cut bullet only if the posting makes it relevant now; the reason it was cut (relevance) does not change because space appeared.

The "third of the page" threshold is the checker's reading of "large empty areas", not a number any source gives. A secondhand statistic about hireability by length has no cited source and is ignored.
