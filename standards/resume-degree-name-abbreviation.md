# Degree name on a resume: spelled out or abbreviated

- **Question:** Does a resume have to spell out the degree ("Bachelor of Science in …") for ATS keyword matching, or can it be shortened ("B.S. in …", "BSc …")?
- **Finding:**
  - **No source requires the full form.** Purdue OWL's own sample entries use the short form ("B.A. in English", "B.S. in Aviation Technology"). Enhancv calls "ATS rejects abbreviations" a myth: "many systems can recognize industry-standard terms", though "some … may ignore them".
  - **The common advice is to give both: full name, abbreviation in parentheses.** Enhancv, Monster and several resume blogs recommend this form, e.g. "Bachelor of Science (BS) in …", so that a recruiter's search for either form matches. Monster: including both can help match keyword searches.
  - **The risk is a mismatch with a filter or search,** not the parser rejecting the resume. A posting or recruiter search for "Bachelor" may miss a bare "B.S.", and the reverse. Blogs say a bare "BS" without periods is sometimes not recognised as a degree by some parsers (stated about Rippling and Greenhouse; not confirmed in vendor docs).
  - **Mirror the posting** when it names the degree a particular way.
  - *Checker's inference:* when space matters, the short form with periods ("B.S. in Information Technology") is the accepted shorter choice and loses little; keep "Bachelor" somewhere on the page when the posting says "Bachelor's degree". Most application forms also ask for the degree in a separate field, which the candidate fills in directly.
