# Resume summary for a new graduate

- **Question:** Does an entry-level resume need a summary, and what should it say? Is a summary that restates the top entry (for example a thesis) good practice?
- **Finding:**
  - **Optional.** Miami University's career centre labels the section "Objective or Summary (Optional)" and cites NACE that there is "no real agreement" among employers on objectives. A summary of Texas A&M's guide says a resume can be excellent with neither. Simplify counts a summary on 24.9% of 3,584 finished resumes from its users with more than one role.
  - **Few readers start there.** Novorésumé's survey of "200+ HR professionals" (undated; page created April 2025): 79.4% look at work experience first and about 10% at the summary or objective. On entry-level resumes, 37.6% see a summary or objective as positive, 48.5% as neutral, 13.9% as adding nothing.
  - **Content: role, evidence, direction.** Miami: "a brief, well-rounded summation of your top skills and accomplishments", matched to the target field; one or two sentences for an objective; avoid trite lines and goals you are not yet qualified for. Simplify: three sentences or fewer, at least one number, no "I"; it "states what the employer gets: your role, your evidence, your direction".
  - **Do not restate the top entry.** Simplify warns that a summary repeating what the top entry already says "only delays" the first read. For new graduates, the value is the thread that ties scattered projects and coursework to a target role. Its test: a first line that could go on a stranger's resume unedited is filler.
  - **When to skip it:** Simplify says a new graduate can skip it when the major, projects and target job already line up on the page. Utah State suggests putting Education directly under the summary when experience is thin.
  - *Checker's inference:* on a one-page resume where the top entry already shows the target work, a summary that lists that entry's methods adds no information. Either drop it, or make it say what the entries below do not: the target role and the thread across the entries, with one piece of evidence.
