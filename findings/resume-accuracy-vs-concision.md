# Accuracy against concision on a resume

- **Question:** When shortening a resume line (an honor, a title, a credential) would make it read wider than the fact, which comes first, accuracy or brevity?
- **Finding:**
  - **Accuracy first; nothing found ranks brevity above it.** SHRM, Dice, Monster and TopResume all treat an inflated credential or an honor not earned as resume fraud, with withdrawn offers and firings as the cited results. Education (degree, dates, GPA, honors) is among the items employers verify, often through a third-party service.
  - **How often education is checked is unclear:** HireRight surveys put it at 32% and 49% in different years (screening-vendor figures, dated).
  - **Concision advice is about cutting padding,** not narrowing facts: "instead of … exaggerating accomplishments, focus on clarity and impact" (Monster).
  - **Omission:** leaving an honor off is not a risk. A cut that makes a credential look like something it was not is the risk. Welcome to the Jungle names omitting that a role was an internship as a common way candidates make experience look stronger, and treats it as part of the faking-it grey zone.
  - *Checker's inference:* the usual fix is placement, not a long phrase everywhere: one line (Education) carries the exact title, and short mentions elsewhere (the summary) use a word that is true without the detail ("program valedictorian", not "IT program valedictorian" when the honor is for one track inside the department).
