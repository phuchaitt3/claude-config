# Popular interview-preparation resources

- **Question:** Which resources do people most often use to prepare for coding and CS-fundamentals interviews, and how do they sequence them?
- **Finding:**
  - *Coding problems.* Three curated LeetCode lists are the most cited: Blind 75 (the original core set), Grind 75 (an offshoot of it) and NeetCode 150 (all of Blind 75 plus 75 more, roughly twice the time). One guide says to start with NeetCode 150 when 8 or more weeks remain and with Blind 75 on a shorter timeline. Another says doing all three has low marginal value because they mostly repeat the same problems.
  - *Study plan.* The Tech Interview Handbook (techinterviewhandbook.org, GitHub `yangshun/tech-interview-handbook`) gives a coding study plan by time left (a 3-month and a 12-week version), per-topic cheatsheets with practice questions, and advice to start mock interviews when about 60% through the plan.
  - *CS fundamentals (OS, networking, DBMS/SQL, OOP).* No single dominant resource surfaced. Listings show many GitHub "CS fundamentals" and interview-question collections (for example a placement-prep collection covering DSA, networks, DBMS and SQL, OOP, OS; a "technical interview megarepo" with OS and system-design folders; a CS cheat sheet with OS and concurrency). The listings gave no star counts, so none can be ranked as the most popular. A general guide says database interviews commonly cover normalization, indexing and writing SQL queries.
  - *Mocks.* The Handbook's advice is to add timed mock interviews from about 60% of the plan; one independent review says the Handbook alone does not prove you can perform under interview conditions, so pair it with a practice platform. (Single review; not tested.)
- **Evidence strength:** *common practice* for the three problem lists and the Handbook (several independent guides agree); *anecdotal* for CS-fundamentals repositories (listing pages only, no popularity data).
