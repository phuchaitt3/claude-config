# How often to check for job openings

- **Question:** how often should one check company career pages, job boards and programme pages for new openings, for regular postings and for annual early-career programmes whose dates are not published?
- **Finding (sourced):**
  - Early application matters, and it is measured for ordinary postings: a St. Louis Fed analysis of postings tracked second by second found 41% of all applications arrive in the first 48 hours and 56% in the first 96 hours (2017 data); a Boston University study (2025) found that the longer a job is posted, the fewer people apply after the first few days. LinkedIn's own claim that 83% of posted jobs get a qualified candidate within 24 hours is vendor marketing with no definition of "qualified".
  - Graduate and early-career schemes commonly assess applications as they arrive and may close before the printed deadline once places fill; the advice in UK graduate-careers sources (Prospects, university careers services, Handshake) is to apply in the first weeks of the window and to track every scheme's opening date. Some schemes have a fixed date (examples: a national health graduate scheme and the Civil Service Fast Stream) and do not close early.
  - Alert and monitoring tools are sold on checking cadence: guidance runs from once a day, at a fixed time, for most searches, to every few minutes only for fast-filling roles at a short list of target employers; the vendors also say to watch a small focused set of employers, not many, and that a company's own career page shows a role before job boards (one vendor says a board can lag 24-72 hours; unverified).
- **Finding (inference, `(inference: findings/job-opening-check-frequency)`; the user's and the checker's reasoning, not a source):** no source was found that says how often to check an annual programme whose dates are unpublished. The working reasoning: split programmes by how consistent their past windows are. A programme whose past windows fall in the same months (two or three cycles) is checked around those months, with a window (start a few weeks before the earliest past opening and keep going until the latest past closing), not on the exact past date, because windows shift by weeks between years. A programme whose past windows do not repeat (spacing varies by months, or a cycle is missing) is checked on a fixed short cycle (weekly to monthly) until a window is published. A checking cost argument: 41% of applications arrive in 48 hours, so for a programme that closes at a fixed date the cost of a late check is small while a window is long (weeks), and large when the programme fills on a rolling basis.
- **Open:** no source for Vietnam (job boards, company career pages, university notice lists); no source for how long early-career programme windows stay open before filling; no study of how much a check interval changes the chance of catching a window.
- **Evidence strength:** *sample or survey* for the 41%/56% figure (one 2017 study; a 2025 university study for the later decline); *common practice* for "apply early to rolling graduate schemes" (several UK careers sources agree); *vendor or single source* for check intervals (alert-tool vendors selling monitoring); *no data found* for the interval for annual programmes with unpublished dates.
