# Several monitors or one

**Question:** Is a multi-monitor desk more common than a single monitor for coders and streamers, and is it better?

**Finding:**

- **Coders at an office mostly use two monitors; at home mostly one.** In one survey of 101 programmers and students (Hebrew University, 2021 preprint), "75% of participants used to working from the office use two monitors (12.5% use one and the other 12.5% use three)"; for people working from home, "only 27%" used more than one ("all the rest use only one"), and none used more than two at home. The paper's own counts for each case are in a garbled figure, so these percentages are the text's, without the denominators.
- **Developers say they want them.** In the same survey, 78.8% of respondents (52 of 66 who answered, read from a figure) said being forced to one monitor would reduce their productivity; 63% of single-monitor users wanted more monitors, 23% of multi-monitor users wanted more still. A 2019 developer survey (CodinGame, seen only in a search snippet) reports 61.1% think two monitors is ideal. An old blog post reports a survey where nobody preferred one monitor over two (seen only in a snippet).
- **Measured productivity is mixed.** The 2021 paper did not measure productivity ("we don't measure productivity directly"); its results are self-reported. The studies it cites differ: about 9% faster with a large display (Microsoft Research, 2003), 6-16% faster (2004), no significant time difference (2011). A University of Utah study funded by a display maker (NEC) found a single large screen matched or beat two smaller ones when the work fits the screen; coverage reports gains of 44% for text tasks and 29% for spreadsheets (press and vendor white papers). A psychologist quoted in the press warns that switching between separate tasks across screens is not good either.
- **One big screen is the main alternative.** Developer and trade-press comparisons say an ultrawide avoids the bezel gap and a dual setup is more flexible in angle and orientation. No reliable count of ultrawide versus dual among developers was found; two product-marketing pages quote a Stack Overflow figure (42% dual, 18% ultrawide) that could not be matched to Stack Overflow's own results, so it is not used here.
- **Streamers: a second screen is the common advice, but no counts exist.** Vendor and affiliate guides say a second monitor is for chat and stream controls, and that one monitor is "possible but not advisable". Some streamers go to three. One comparison says many start with two and move to an ultrawide. None cites a survey.
- **The "coders and streamers prefer several" impression is supported as a preference and as office practice for coders; it is not supported as a measured productivity gain, and for streamers it rests on advice, not data.**

**Evidence strength:** *sample or survey* for the coder figures (one survey, 101 people, recruited through the authors' personal ties, so "predominantly from large companies" and similar backgrounds); *vendor or single source* for the productivity studies (some display-maker funded, 2003-2011); *no data found* for streamers' monitor counts.

The Stack Overflow Developer Survey was not checked directly for monitor questions (the search found none). Nothing found for single versus several monitors on a laptop-plus-desktop desk, on eye strain or posture, or on surveys of streamers.
