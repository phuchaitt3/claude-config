# Bold text in a resume

- **Question:** Do guides recommend bolding on a resume, which parts, and does bold hurt or help an applicant tracking system (ATS)?
- **Finding:**
  - **Bold is recommended, sparingly.** Career sources agree that bold helps a skimming reader when it marks a few elements and loses its effect when overused. Common targets: section headings, job or project titles, and at most one or two standout items per role (one key metric, or a short lead-in at the start of a bullet). Bolding whole sentences is advised against (Weekday, The Muse, Let's Eat Grandma, Jobscan search summaries).
  - **Bolding technical terms in bullets is allowed but contested.** Some guides say bolding languages or frameworks (Python, Java) gives a skimming recruiter a visual anchor; the same guides warn that heavy bolding becomes clutter, and that a term that does not fit the role is wasted bold. One result frames it as "yay or nay" with no rule. No source tested whether it changes who is shortlisted.
  - **Consistency is the one firm rule:** if job titles are bold in one entry, every entry's title is bold (Weekday).
  - **ATS: bold is not a parsing risk.** Several guides say most ATS parse text without regard to bold or italics, so bold neither helps nor hurts keyword matching. The documented parsing risks are structural: text boxes, headers and footers, tables, multi-column layouts. Underline is the one style one source flags as not always ATS-friendly. Keywords count more when they appear in context inside experience bullets than when only listed.
  - *Checker's inference:* a resume that bolds headings and titles follows the common practice. Bolding words inside bullets is a taste choice with no evidence either way; if used, keep it to a few relevant terms per entry and apply the same rule in every entry. A master resume that holds a note or tag in a different style should keep bold for that style distinct from content bold.
- **Evidence strength:** *common practice* for headings and titles plus "sparingly" (many career sites agree); *vendor or single source* for each claim about ATS and bold (no ATS vendor tests bold); *no data found* on any effect of bolding keywords on interviews or shortlisting.
