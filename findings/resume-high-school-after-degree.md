# High school on a resume after a university degree

- **Question:** Once a candidate holds (or is finishing) a university degree, do people keep a prestigious or selective high school (e.g. a specialised school for the gifted) in the Education section?
- **Finding:**
  - Default: drop it. US career sites (Monster, Jobscan, Simplify, Enhancv, TopResume) agree that a degree makes the high school diploma redundant; it is kept mainly when it is the highest education or the candidate is still a student. None of these general guides treats prestige as an exception.
  - Prestige exception: weak and time-limited. Forum and admissions-advice sources (CollegeVine) say an especially prestigious or competitive high school may stay for a few years after university, then go as work experience builds up. No hiring or recruiter source backs this exception.
  - Vietnam: Vietnamese career sites (CareerLink, Glints VN, Cake, VietNamWorks) describe the fresher's Education section as the university, major, dates and GPA. Specialised-school details appear only in advice for current high school students. No source covers a gifted school on a graduate's CV.
  - *Checker's inference:* a selective high school adds signal mainly when (a) the reader knows it (domestic or regional employers), (b) it carries an achievement the role values (a national olympiad prize, a specialisation that matches the posting), or (c) there is room. On a page-limited, tailored resume for a technical role, it is usually the first Education line to cut. A master resume may keep it. Something the school shows (e.g. English specialisation) is better carried by a direct proof (a test score) where one exists.
