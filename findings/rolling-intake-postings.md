# Rolling-intake ("apply any time") postings

- **Question:** are postings and programme pages with no deadline legitimate and open all year, or listings nobody took down, and how does a candidate tell?
- **Finding (sourced):**
  - An open-ended posting kept up on purpose is a recognised practice, called an evergreen posting: the employer hires for the role continuously or keeps a pipeline for turnover and growth. Sources separate it from a ghost job by intent and disclosure: an evergreen posting is open to real hires and says so; a ghost job has no intent to hire (pipeline-building, market-testing, signalling growth). Several sources say it is the same old talent-pool idea; the dispute is honesty. A recruiting-software vendor advises closing and re-creating an evergreen posting about every three months to keep it current.
  - Ghost listings are common but the size is disputed and the figures measure different things: Greenhouse (US, UK and Germany, 2024) found 18-22% of postings in one quarter were ghost jobs, up to 34% of UK roles in its employer survey; a job-site analysis of US job-openings data (June 2025) found about 30% of openings never produced a hire (an outcome, not an intent); a resume-vendor survey (2024) found 39% of hiring managers admit their company posted fake listings; an Indeed trends expert said inactive postings are not a prevalent problem. All are vendor or trade-press figures with undisclosed methods.
  - A rolling intake is not a promise of a reply: pages of this kind commonly say they answer only suitable applicants (seen on one Vietnamese fresher-programme page, 2026).
- **Finding (inference, `(inference: findings/rolling-intake-postings)`; not a source):** signs that a rolling page is live rather than forgotten, since no source gives a test: the page carries a recent "updated" date or a numbered cohort or squad; it states why there is no deadline (continuous selection until places are filled) and what the next season is; its entry process (test, interview) is described in current detail; the posting's other pages (news, social posts, application form) are current. Signs of a forgotten page: the only date is a past deadline or a past year in the title, a notice copied to a third-party site with a stale date, a form that still accepts applications after a printed deadline, no description of the process.
- **Open:** no source covers Vietnam: nothing found on how common evergreen or ghost postings are on Vietnamese job boards or company career pages, and nothing on whether Vietnamese fresher programmes that run "continuously" reply to most applicants. A Vietnamese-language search (2026-11-20) returned only job-board listings.
- **Evidence strength:** *vendor or single source* for the percentages (undisclosed methods, US and UK samples); *common practice* for "evergreen postings are legitimate" (several trade sources agree); *no data found* for Vietnam.
