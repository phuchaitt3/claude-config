# Error log reread before a test

- **Question:** Does keeping a short list of your own recurring slips (errors fixed by a quick mental correction, not deep study) and rereading it just before a test help, and what does research say about correcting errors?
- **Finding:**
  - *No direct test of the technique found.* Searches for a controlled study or meta-analysis of student-kept error logs ("wrong-answer notebook", "mistake notebook") found only practitioner guides for exam preparation (law, medical, GRE, GMAT) and one conference paper on a "Wrong Answer Note" in school maths that classifies errors and does not report learning gains. The nearest experiments test feedback shown by a platform on common wrong answers, not a log the student keeps. Support is indirect.
  - *Errors are corrected well at first, then fade.* In a study summarised by a university news item, immediate retesting corrected 86% of errors; a week later 56%. When the right answer was forgotten, the more confident the original error, the more likely it came back. The authors suggest extra chances to retrieve the correct answer keep corrections from reverting. Single study of 50 undergraduates on basic science questions, read through a search summary, not the paper.
  - *Why errors get corrected is not settled.* The hypercorrection effect (confident errors corrected more often after feedback) is explained by surprise at the mismatch in one dissertation; a later experiment found low-confidence errors on trick questions were corrected just as well and that memory for the feedback predicted correction better than confidence or surprise. Separate experiments, read through a search summary.
  - *Practitioner guides agree on form:* keep the log short and focused on recurring causes (count causes, not total mistakes), re-solve a logged item from scratch instead of reading the answer, and retest it after a gap. Their research citations are the general testing and spacing papers, not studies of the log; one GRE guide cites "studies" with none named (unverified).
  - *(inference: findings/study-error-log-pretest-review)* A last-day reread of a short list of slips fits the "extra exposure keeps corrections from reverting" result and costs little, so the practice is reasonable. It is rereading, which `findings/effective-study-techniques` rates low as a main method, so it works as a final cue on top of tested recall, not instead of it. Covering the correction and recalling it before checking would bring it in line with retrieval practice (inference from the two entries, not a tested result).
- **Evidence strength:** *anecdotal* for the error log itself (guides, no controlled study found); *vendor or single source* for the durability numbers (one study, 50 students).
