# How often SQL is asked in CS and AI technical tests and interviews

- **Question:** How often is SQL asked in technical tests and interviews for CS and entry-level AI or ML roles, compared with data roles?
- **Finding:**
  - *Data roles: SQL is the most common technical topic.* An analysis of public interview reports (about 22,000, Jan 2023 to Jun 2025) is quoted in search snippets as finding SQL in about 39% of data-engineer reports, 25% of data-analyst reports and 20% of data-scientist reports, and the most mentioned technical skill across data roles. A second platform reports SQL in roughly 95% of data-engineer, 85% of analytics-engineer and 80% of data-scientist loops (self-reported loops, not a representative sample).
  - *ML engineer: rare in the same report data.* The same snippet puts SQL in about 4% of ML-engineer reports. If that figure holds, a plain "SQL is always asked" does not apply to ML-engineer roles. This is the key number and it was not verified on the page (see Limits).
  - *Prep guides disagree with that figure.* Vendor guides say SQL appears for ML engineer roles because feature engineering runs on it, and that entry-level depth is basic: aggregates, joins, `GROUP BY` versus `HAVING`, with window functions as the next step. This is a vendor claim, not measured.
  - *Vietnam and entry-level AI tests.* Anonymous candidate reports for FPT Software mention SQL in several technical rounds (a definition question, "sql problem and DSA", a data quiz of about 30 questions), but they cover software and data-engineer roles. Nothing was found for Viettel or VNG, and no published syllabus for an entry-level AI test lists SQL.
  - *Software engineering.* No systematic data; one backend engineer says SQL was never asked, another commenter says it was a large part of corporate-IT interviews. Anecdotes only.
  - *Practical reading (inference, not a source claim):* SQL is likely in a test for a role with data, analytics or database in its title or posting; for a pure AI or ML role it is plausible but less likely than coding and ML basics. Check the posting for "SQL" or "database" before deciding how long to spend; cap it at the basics (`GROUP BY`/`HAVING`, joins) when the posting does not name it. See also `findings/entry-level-ml-interview-emphasis`.
- **Evidence strength:** *anecdotal to common practice.* Two platform analyses of self-reported or public interview reports (one headline set of figures, not independently checked) plus prep-site claims and anonymous candidate reports; no survey of employers.

"Mentioned in a report" is not the same as "asked in a test", and reports are self-selected, mostly US, mostly for data roles. The platforms sell SQL prep. No Vietnamese entry-level AI test data. Review again before relying on the 4% figure.
