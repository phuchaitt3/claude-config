# Does a referral skip the resume and ATS round?

- **Question:** does an employee referral let a candidate skip the resume and ATS screen, and what does it do instead?
- **Finding:**
  - **Skipping the screen: no source shows it as a rule.** The claim "referrals bypass the ATS" appears only in career-coaching and referral-marketplace blogs, always stated as general practice with no named company, study or data. Two ATS vendor pages checked (Greenhouse's Referrals FAQ and its auto-reject documentation) say nothing about referred candidates being exempt from auto-reject rules or from the first review stage. Auto-reject is a rule the employer sets (for example on yes/no application questions), so whether a referral escapes it depends on that company's setup. (inference: findings/referral-skips-resume-screen, from the vendor pages' silence plus the blogs' lack of evidence; not a sourced statement that referrals are never exempt.)
  - **What does happen, by documentation:** the ATS records the source "Referral" and credits the referrer (Greenhouse documents this for its referral dashboard). That tag is what a company can use to route or prioritise a referred application; whether it does is the company's choice. A recruiter screen after a referral is still commonly reported (a Blind thread and a headhunter column, anecdotal).
  - **What referrals do to the odds (sourced):** an academic study of one large US firm's applicant data finds referred candidates are more likely to be hired, start with higher pay, and stay longer (Brown, Setren and Topa, 2016). Vendor and blog figures are far larger (about 30% of referred candidates hired against about 7% from job boards; referrals about 7% of applicants but 30-50% of hires), but they are repeated across secondary pages and the primary reports could not be found, so treat them as direction, not rates. Share of hires from referrals ranges from about 10% (SHRM 2024 program data) to over 30% (SHRM, 2016 data) depending on year and population.
  - **Recruiter voice (anecdotal, one thread):** in an r/recruiting thread (about 6 years old, 25 comments, comments of 1-7 upvotes), a recruiter for three large tech companies says none had an ATS that screens or auto-rejects on words, and a human actions every online application, ordered by date; a referral form there asks how the referrer knows the candidate, with an option for "don't really know this person". Another hiring-side commenter says a referral with no real working connection is treated like any other applicant, and the referrer is viewed with more caution afterwards. A third says most ATS do not screen the way candidates assume, and usually all resumes get looked at. No commenter says a referral skips the resume review; the value attributed to a referral is a trusted human vouching. The poster's own premise that referrals bypass the filter is a belief, not evidence.
  - **Practical reading** (inference: findings/referral-skips-resume-screen): expect a referral, when the referrer really knows you, to get the resume read by a person and possibly sooner, not to remove the resume from the process. The resume still has to pass the human read, and a recruiter or hiring-manager screen may follow.
- **Evidence strength:** *anecdotal* for the bypass claim and the vendor numbers; *vendor or single source* for the ATS documentation (silence, not a statement); one peer-reviewed study (single firm) for the higher hire odds.

- **Limits:** the search tools reach no Reddit, so the community evidence is one thread pasted by the user, about 6 years old and small, with one recruiter speaking only for large tech companies. No source gives how many companies exempt referrals from screening. Only Greenhouse documentation was checked; Lever, Workday and other ATS products may differ. The 30% versus 7% figures could not be traced to the primary Jobvite report. The study covers one US firm and says nothing on Vietnam or entry-level hiring.
