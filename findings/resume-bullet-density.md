# Resume bullet density: when is an entry a "wall of text"?

- **Question:** An entry has seven bullets, most two lines and one three lines, each with several numbers. Is that acceptable, or do guides say to trim or simplify?
- **Finding:**
  - **Dense blocks are advised against, with soft limits.** Guides say bullets run 1–2 lines (about 15–30 words; one tool says 12–20), and that bullets over three lines are often skipped. A dense block looks harder to read than it is, and recruiters move on (Work It Daily; Resumeble on cognitive load; Great Resumes Fast on text density).
  - **Bullets per entry: 3–5 is the common range, 3–6 appears too.** Simplify's count of 11,819 jobs gives a median of 4 (findings/resume-tailoring-project-context). Guides give the most relevant entry the most bullets (5–6). Seven is above every range found, though one thesis entry is the most relevant entry on a research-leaning resume.
  - **White space helps skimming, but a large empty area signals thin experience** (findings/resume-one-page-fill). Squeezing to fill a page works against the first point.
  - **No source gives a limit on numbers per bullet.** Guides say numbers help; one vendor tool suggests a metric in about 80% of bullets. The "one idea per bullet" rule in the project already caps this.
  - *Checker's inference:* the fix guides point to is fewer and shorter bullets, not smaller type or tighter margins. A bullet over two lines, or an entry over about five bullets, should carry a reason to stay (it is the posting's core skill; it holds a result found nowhere else). The cheap trims are, in order: drop the least relevant bullet for the posting; shorten a three-line bullet to two by moving setup detail (hardware, subset, method name) to the interview; keep the number, drop the explanation of how it was reached.
  - *Checker's inference, trim order (updated 2026-11-15):* when a bullet carries a measured range across setups, trim method detail first, then hardware names; keep the condition that bounds the claim (input or output length, sample). Hardware goes to the interview unless the claim is about that hardware (findings/resume-benchmark-context; corrected 2026-11-15, an earlier version said to keep hardware).
  - *Checker's inference:* in a master resume that is never sent, density is not a defect (findings/resume-master-subtractive-tailoring). It matters in a variant, where the reader is real.
- **Evidence strength:** *common practice* for the 1–2 line limit and the 3–5 bullet range (many career sites agree); *sample or survey* for the median of 4 (one vendor, one sample); *no data found* for any effect of density on interview rates.
