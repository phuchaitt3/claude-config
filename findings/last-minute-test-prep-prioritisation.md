# Last-minute technical test preparation: highest-yield topics and sample questions first

- **Question:** With very little time left before a technical test (multiple choice plus live coding, entry-level AI or software role), is it sound to study only the highest-frequency topics, and to collect sample or past questions first to see what is asked?
- **Finding:**
  - *Prioritising by frequency is the common advice.* Last-minute guides say to spend the time on patterns that recur (arrays and strings, hash maps, two pointers, sliding window, trees) and not to start hard topics cold; dynamic programming and backtracking are named as taking real time to absorb. They also say not to learn new algorithms in the final days, to start from a list of the topics that cause hesitation, and to add timed mocks. Several guides say the prep need not be full-time.
  - *Frequency lists are popularity signals, not measured interview counts.* Community posts agree that arrays, strings and hash tables dominate coding problems. A search for the underlying statistics found no official frequency data; the percentages beside some lists look like acceptance rates or have no stated method (checker's reading of the search summary).
  - *For multiple-choice screening tests at entry level, reports list CS subjects first.* Candidate reports and vendor pages for fresher assessments name data structures and algorithms (arrays, strings, hashing, searching, sorting, time and space complexity), DBMS and SQL (normalisation, indexing, transactions), operating systems (processes, threads, deadlocks, scheduling), networking (TCP/UDP, OSI), OOP and output-based programming questions. One source says to do the data-structure topics before OOP, DBMS and SQL. AI or ML assessments cover a smaller block: ML techniques, evaluation metrics, NLP, neural-network basics; one company's AI assessment is AI literacy, not model maths. The mix varies by company.
  - *Using past or sample questions to direct study is supported by learning guidance.* A meta-analysis of 118 studies (reported through a university summary) found practice tests beat other study methods. University learning centres advise taking a practice test early as a baseline, studying, then retesting; and say past exams show the level of detail expected, how topics are grouped, and which areas are weak. Mixed question formats helped memory more than a single format (secondary summary).
  - *Target-test reports are missing (added the same day, second search round).* Searches for candidate reports of one Vietnamese company's residency test, and of AI-engineer fresher tests at four Vietnamese firms, found no test content. The closest hits: a 2025 Glassdoor report of a software-fresher interview at one of them (networking including IPv6, a LeetCode-medium problem), a 2022 report (pointers, arrays, AVL trees, sorting), and a company page saying its fresher programme has an on-site entry test. Lists of "most asked" OS, networking and DBMS questions agree on process vs thread, TCP vs UDP, OSI, normalisation and ACID, but name no authoritative ranking.
  - *Data-unit names per OSI layer (added 2026-11-23, one standard search).* Prep and explainer pages give the same names and call them a common interview and exam question: bits (physical), frame (data link), packet (network), segment (transport; datagram for UDP), with the chain "a TCP segment rides inside an IP packet, which rides inside an Ethernet frame". None of them measures how often it is asked of freshers; the guides warn that beginners use packet, frame and segment interchangeably. Per-layer detail beyond the names (header fields, every protocol per layer) has no frequency data at all.
  - *Other VNG hiring, forum and wiki reports (added 2026-11-24, two standard searches; search summaries only).* No report of the VNG AI Builder Residency test exists (a new programme; the search found none). Older material on VNG's fresher and intern hiring: a vnoi.info wiki account describes the first-round entry test as logic and basic programming, no domain knowledge needed, drawn from a secret question bank that VNG updates often (one person's account, possibly old); Voz threads about VNG technical interviews say interns are mostly asked OOP, data structures and algorithms, SQL and their language, that live coding depends on the interviewer, and that fresher questions are not very hard; a Zalo Tech Fresher page says that programme's entry test is held offline at the VNG campus. Whether VNG reuses one question bank across its programmes is the user's working assumption, not something any source states. Facebook groups were not searched (login-walled; no automation).
  - *VNG fresher test on Voz, and a proctored-assessment report (added 2026-11-24, three searches limited to voz.vn, one page read in full).* A Voz thread on the VNG fresher test (opened March 2021, last reply September 2023, about 154 replies) is the main source. Read in full, page 1: a multiple-choice test of a few dozen questions in the style of online C quizzes (predict the output, find the error, find the compile error), mostly data structures, one or two OOP questions, no SQL, then an English test, then an interview; one poster says the pass mark is roughly 60 to 70 percent and the interview was harder. Search summaries only (pages 2 to 7 returned HTTP 403 to the reader): 25 to 30 questions in 60 minutes, sorting-algorithm complexity, tree traversals, stack, queue, linked list, BFS, bit operators and OOP the most named, and two AI questions added from August 2023 (one poster, content not given). All of this concerns software fresher programmes from 2021 to 2023, not the AI Builder Residency. A pasted Facebook comment about a Shopee online assessment says another tab or the screen sleeping was flagged and three flags ended the test (one anonymous report).
  - *VNG fresher test on Voz, read in full (added 2026-11-24, after `webx` was fixed; pages 3 to 7 of the thread).* Anecdotal reports, software and game fresher programmes, 2022 to 2023: April 2022, 25 multiple-choice questions, "all data structures and algorithms", mostly algorithm complexity (best and worst case), tree traversals and code reading with blanks; August 2023, 30 questions in 60 minutes spread across data structures and algorithms, plus two AI questions (content not given), some arithmetic with big numbers and no calculator; a candidate's 2022 recall lists sorting algorithms as the most asked, then trees, stack and queue, linked lists, BFS order, bit operators and recursion; round 2 is an interview with algorithms and some design. One thread also reports an interview that asked only networking (HTTP and HTTPS, OSI, TCP/IP, TCP and UDP, DNS). About 50 hired from about 1000 test takers in 2022 (one poster). Live coding in the first round was asked about once and never answered. Two readers called posting detailed test questions unfair. A former-employee post (March 2022, unverifiable) says the fresher interview re-checks university theory, operating systems and computer networks, with no LeetCode medium and no system design, and that interviews were in Vietnamese; a candidate describes about 1000 people in one hall sitting a multiple-choice test of code reading, algorithms and some object-oriented theory. All eight pages of the thread are now read; these replace the earlier "search summary only" caveats.
  - *VNG fresher reports on Glassdoor, read in full (added 2026-11-25; replaces the same-day summary-based line).* Four anonymous "Fresher Software Engineer" reviews, read from a saved page. March 2025 (Ho Chi Minh City, offer accepted): a test of 30 questions in 30 minutes, mostly CS fundamentals, then an interview of about 2 hours with 2 LeetCode problems (1 easy, 1 medium); the one listed question is IPv6 networking. April 2024: a paper test on basic algorithms, data structures and OOP, then an interview with LeetCode-medium problems, OOP and design patterns; no database. February 2019: a paper test, then an interview with team lead and HR; the listed question names data structures, networking, operating systems, databases and 2 programming exercises. August 2018: a 100-minute test on networking, English, social and operating systems; the listed question is pointers, arrays, AVL trees and sorting (an earlier line of this entry wrongly attached that list to 2025). These conflict with a 2022 unverifiable post that said no LeetCode medium. Four anonymous posts, one programme, not independent confirmation of question content; no new Voz, Reddit or Blind report for 2024 to 2026 was found. Effect: first-round content is CS fundamentals broader than data structures in some years; a medium coding problem in the interview is plausible. (standard: findings/last-minute-test-prep-prioritisation, checked 2026-11-25)
  - *Limit of the sample approach.* The guidance assumes the practice test resembles the real one. A sample set from other companies is a proxy for the target test's topic mix, not its blueprint; if the test's own content areas are withheld until the session, a proxy set can only rank topics by how often similar tests ask them.
  - *Inference (checker's own):* a one-to-two-hour pass of sampling questions from several independent sources, tallying topics, then ordering study by tally and by the learner's own baseline errors, follows from the points above; no source tests that exact procedure.
- **Evidence strength:** *common practice* for frequency-first and no-new-hard-topics advice (several independent prep guides agree, all commercial); *anecdotal* for the topic mix of fresher MCQ tests (Glassdoor-type reports and vendor pages, no sample size); *common practice* for practice-test-first (meta-analysis plus university guidance).
