# Submit now or keep polishing the resume

- **Question:** A resume is accurate and tailored but some optional keywords or skills are still missing. Is it better to submit now, or to delay to close the gaps?
- **Finding:**
  - **Practitioner advice leans one way: submit, and keep the polish for later.** Career-centre and coaching pieces say hours or days of re-editing hurt more than help, suggest applying to a couple of roles before reworking the resume again, and say the saved time is better spent on networking and follow-up. They still expect basic accuracy (spelling, grammar, correct names) and separate cosmetic perfectionism (fonts, synonyms) from substantive content. (inference: findings/resume-speed-vs-polish; practitioner opinion, no controlled study)
  - **Timing evidence is thin.** The one academic source (University of Chicago, 66 million applications linked to about 8 million postings) shows that many applications arrive in a posting's first days (39% within 48 hours); it does not show that early applicants get more interviews. The large multipliers ("8x within four days", "2 to 3x within 48 hours") come from job-alert sites and blogs, with the studies unnamed or untraceable. An applicant-tracking vendor's report calls the early-bird advantage a belief it had not yet tested. So: direction plausible, size unknown.
  - **Referrals add to the case for not delaying, and set a floor.** Referred candidates convert better (see `referral-skips-resume-screen`), but a Lever study found about 30% of referrals were considered underqualified, so the resume is still read for fit and claims. That favours an accurate resume over a keyword-complete one. (inference)
  - **No source weighs "missing optional keywords" against speed.** Nothing found measures how much one absent nice-to-have tool lowers the chance of a screen or interview. Existing entries say listing a tool without work behind it is the larger risk (`resume-keyword-stuffing`) and that readiness means the checklist passes and every claim can be defended, with refinement past that having diminishing returns (`resume-submission-readiness`).
  - **Practical reading** (inference: findings/resume-speed-vs-polish): if the readiness checklist passes and every printed claim has a source, submit when a deadline or referral window requires it. Missing nice-to-have terms are a reason to keep working after submitting (a project, then an update if the portal allows), not a reason to hold the application. A missing must-have, a false claim or a broken file is a reason to delay.
- **Evidence strength:** *anecdotal / practitioner opinion* for the advice; *sample (single academic study)* for when people apply; *vendor or unverified* for the interview multipliers; *no data found* for the effect of missing optional keywords.
