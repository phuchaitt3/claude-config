# Where a top academic honor goes on a resume

- **Question:** Where does a top academic honor (valedictorian, Latin honors, class rank) go on a new graduate's resume so that it is seen, and may it go in the summary?
- **Finding:**
  - **Default: the Education section, next to the degree.** Purdue OWL lists "academic honors or graduated with distinction" as Education content. Resume Worded calls Education "the best place for academic honors and cumulative GPA", listed below the degree. Career blogs agree, with examples like "BA …, Valedictorian, Magna Cum Laude" on the degree line or on its own line below it.
  - **Education goes near the top when it is the strongest qualification.** Purdue OWL: for recent graduates, Education "may be a major focus for recruiters"; put it near the top if it is your strongest qualification.
  - **For more emphasis: a separate Honors/Awards section.** Resume Worded suggests one "if you want to shine a spotlight on any particularly impressive honors", with the most impressive first, naming the issuer and date. One blog notes that an Honors section placed above Education stands out more. These suit several honors better than one.
  - **In the summary: allowed as its evidence.** No source found names the summary as the place for an academic honor. Simplify's summary advice ("your role, your evidence, your direction", at least one number) admits a top honor as the evidence; see `resume-summary-entry-level`.
  - **Shelf life:** blogs say to drop school honors after about two years of work, or once stronger credentials exist.
  - *Checker's inference:* when an honor sits inside an italic sub-line it is easy to miss. Typography (its own line, or the bold degree line) makes it visible without a new section. If a summary is kept, leading with the honor gives the summary a job the rest of the page does not already do.
