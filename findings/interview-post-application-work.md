# Interviews: work done after the resume was submitted

- **Question:** After submitting a resume, may a candidate improve a project and talk about the new work in interviews even though the resume does not show it, or must they stay within what the resume says? Is it worth building more before the deadline?
- **Finding:**
  - **Interviews are not limited to the resume, and recent work is a normal thing to bring up.** Guides for the "tell me something that is not on your resume" question suggest one recent accomplishment or project tied to the role, kept to one topic and not over-explained. One adds that a resume is probably at least a month out of date, so a recent win may not be on it yet (Fairygodboss; read through a search summary, the page itself returned 403).
  - **What is not allowed is changing facts, not adding to them.** Interviewers keep the submitted version. In one interviewer-reported case, a candidate brought a "new" resume with different end dates that closed a gap, and the interviewers, holding the original, treated it poorly. A real addition is fine; a reworded past result or altered dates is not (PrepLounge thread). Lying or inflating in an interview is described as risky because interviewers test it: they use the resume as the script, and answers that do not match raise a red flag (Vault, ACCA, Robert Half). Skills inflation is usually exposed in interviews, skills tests or early on the job. Offers are withdrawn mainly over verifiable facts (degrees, dates, titles); one legal commentary says the misstatement must be serious, not "mere puffery".
  - **Whatever is raised must be defensible in depth.** Resume-based screening is a walk-through of architecture, choices, a hard bug, what you would improve, and how a number was measured, each answer followed by "why?". A simpler project known inside out beats a complex-sounding one barely understood (DesignGurus, PrachHub, NamasteDev). See `interview-project-depth`.
  - **Sending an updated resume after applying:** sources advise against sending an unprompted revision during the application stage; wait for an interview invitation, or ask the recruiter whether an update is welcome. Some portals show employers only the latest upload, others do not allow replacing it (Zety, Idealist, PrepLounge, bestjobs support; through search summaries).
  - *Checker's inference (no source states these directly):* (a) "everything on the resume was true on the day it was sent" follows from the dates anecdote and the red-flag sources; (b) "present new work as new, 'since I applied…'" is the safe form of the guides' advice about recent work, because it cannot be mistaken for a changed resume; (c) "keep building shows initiative, which a residency looks for" is plausible but no source says so for residencies, so treat it as untested; (d) "pick work that doubles as interview practice" is the checker's suggestion, not a sourced practice.
  - **Before the deadline:** no source compares building more features with finishing the application. `resume-submission-readiness` (checker's inference there) says refinement past "every item passes and every claim is defensible" has diminishing returns against the deadline.
- **Evidence strength:** *anecdotal*. Career sites, prep vendors, interviewer-reported forum posts and a few HR or legal commentaries agree in direction; no survey or study was found, and nothing on the specific "improved after submitting" case.
